Research Experience

  • March 2023 – Present
    Graduate Researcher, Computational Biophotonics Lab
    Johns Hopkins University · Advisor, Nicholas Durr, Ph.D.
    • Built a smartphone-based nailfold capillaroscopy system using green-LED back-illumination and a reverse-lens optical relay; characterized at 25 lp/mm resolution with field-of-view sufficient to resolve 20 μm capillary loops.
    • Worked onsite with Samsung Mobile imaging engineers to characterize the production smartphone camera stack and proposed an exposure-controlled, auto-focus-swept acquisition pipeline that satisfied Samsung's stock-camera constraints while preserving capillary contrast across skin tones.
    • Designed and 3D-printed an ergonomic finger fixture that reduced motion-artifact rejection rates and improved frame-to-frame registration in longitudinal recordings.
    • Implemented an automated focus-sweep routine (focus value 0.0–1.0) to capture capillary loops at multiple focal planes within a single acquisition.
    • Built a Python pipeline (NumPy, OpenCV) for Beer–Lambert hemoglobin estimation from capillary image stacks, including background flat-field correction and vessel segmentation.
    • Ran a pilot study across light- and dark-skinned subjects, identified an exposure-compensation regime that preserves capillary contrast in darker skin, and defined a skin-tone–aware acquisition protocol now used as the lab default.
    • Currently extending the pipeline with Monte Carlo (mcxyz) forward models of light transport in skin to build calibration tables that map observed contrast and apparent vessel width to capillary depth and hemoglobin concentration.
  • May 2025 – Present
    Graduate Researcher, Artificial Intelligence for Engineering and Medicine Lab
    Johns Hopkins University · Advisor, Rama Chellappa, Ph.D.
    • Led design of a unified adaptive-inference framework for DeiT-S Vision Transformers (PyTorch, timm) that combines token reduction and early exit for medical image classification.
    • Integrated four token-reduction strategies (EViT, Top-K, Random, ToMe) and trained a lightweight CNN redundancy predictor with dataset-specific confidence thresholds to decide per-image whether to prune tokens or exit early.
    • Built training, validation, and profiling pipelines across five medical-image datasets, running all FLOPs/latency analyses, ablation studies, and redundancy-vs-accuracy trade-off curves.
    • Co-first-author paper accepted to MIDL 2025.
